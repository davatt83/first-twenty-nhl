What these metrics tell us

Brier = average squared probability error, 0 is perfect. Log loss penalizes confident incorrect predictions more heavily; lower is better. Sample sizes here are small, and no independent future-season holdout exists. Better historical Brier does not guarantee profitable wagers.

How these estimates are calculated

We compute each team's first-period scoring and concession rates at the correct venue. Estimated home team goals = average of home-team 1P GF/game and away-team 1P GA/game. Away expectation is the average of away-team 1P GF/game and home-team 1P GA/game. The original arithmetic Poisson model remains the baseline. The new matchup model also uses league-normalized offensive and defensive goal-rate ratios, shrunk toward the league mean. The hybrid conservatively blends 70% of the original baseline with 30% of a pseudo-game-smoothed, outcome-adjusted matchup estimate from actual first-period Over 1.5 outcomes. P(2+) = 1 − e^(−λ)(1+λ). Combined mode adds each current game at 2.5× weight versus a 2025–26 game. The 2026–27-only model uses an explicitly disclosed neutral fixed scoring prior (8 pseudo-games per score-side) because early-season sample sizes are tiny; it does not use 2025–26 team results. The Validation tab checks forecasts in calendar order; same-day scores are not available when predicting other games that day. The main displayed price uses the original arithmetic Poisson benchmark because the experimental alternatives did not demonstrate better accuracy in the 2026–27 walk-forward checks. Full 2025–26 game-by-game records are now embedded, so actual historical Over 1.5 rates are available. The hybrid remains visible as an experimental comparator. True goal totals may not be Poisson-distributed, and these prices are not proven profitable.
